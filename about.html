Big-company IT discipline, sized for your business.

ClearSeat was founded by Carlo Baptista, who spent 25 years making sure complicated technology delivered in some of the most demanding organisations in Africa.

Why I started ClearSeat.

In a bank, nothing in technology happens without someone accountable for it. Every supplier has a contract with clear promises. Every project has an owner, a budget and a date. Every month, someone reports on whether those promises were kept.

Then I look at the businesses my friends run. Good businesses, with several branches and loyal customers. They have a till system from one supplier, bookings from another, phones from a third, and an IT support company that fixes things when they break. Nobody owns the whole picture. The owner ends up doing it at night.

They don't need a full-time IT executive, and they couldn't justify one. They need the same thinking for a few hours a month, from someone who has nothing to sell them. That is ClearSeat.

Carlo Baptista, founder

What 25 years in banking taught me, and why it matters to you.

  • How to hold suppliers to their word.

    I have managed technology contracts and service agreements where a missed promise had real consequences. I know which clauses matter, how suppliers negotiate and how to get what you are paying for.

    For you: no more refereeing, and no more renewals you didn't see coming.

  • How to deliver change that can't be allowed to fail.

    I worked on the separation of Absa from Barclays, a multi-year programme involving several consulting firms and regulators in a number of African countries and the UK. Since then I have led the oversight of a large portfolio of strategic projects.

    For you: a new branch or a new system delivered on time, while the business keeps trading.

  • What good looks like.

    I have spent my career inside organisations with mature ways of running IT services, managing risk and reporting to a board. Most of it can be scaled down to a single page.

    For you: a simple, regular view of your IT that you can actually read.

  • What it is to pay for it yourself.

    I have owned and run a small business of my own. I know that every rand spent on IT is a rand not spent on something else.

    For you: advice that respects your budget.
